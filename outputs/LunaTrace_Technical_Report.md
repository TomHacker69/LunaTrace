### Executive Summary
Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) and doubly shadowed cold traps ($T < 100\text{ K}$) at the Lunar South Pole harbor significant volatile water ice reserves. However, lunar radar data exhibits a severe ambiguity: **elevated Circular Polarization Ratio ($\text{CPR} > 1.0$) is generated by both low-loss coherent ice crystal scattering (CBOE) and wavelength-scale rocky ejecta boulders**.

**LunaTrace** solves this fundamental remote sensing challenge by building an end-to-end, reproducible, 6-phase geospatial engineering pipeline using **Chandrayaan-2 Dual Frequency SAR (DFSAR)** hybrid polarimetry (L-band and S-band), **LOLA Digital Elevation Models (DEM)**, and solar/Earth horizon ray-tracing models:
1. **DFSAR Polarimetric Processing:** Extracts Stokes parameters ($S_1 - S_4$), CPR, Degree of Polarization (DoP, $m$), and Raney $m-\chi$ 3-component decomposition.
2. **Ice Detection Framework:** Resolves CPR ambiguity using dual-frequency depth profiling ($\Delta \text{CPR} = \text{CPR}_L - \text{CPR}_S$), external rocky ejecta control masking, slope/boulder hazard penalties, and a continuous probabilistic scoring model ($0.0 - 1.0$).
3. **Terrain & Illumination Modeling:** Derives 20 m slope, aspect, RMS roughness, azimuthal horizon ray-tracing for solar illumination over the 18.6-year precession cycle, and Earth direct line-of-sight (LOS) communications under lunar libration.
4. **Landing Site Selection:** Executes a Weighted Multi-Criteria Evaluation (MCE) under hard engineering constraints ($\theta \le 5^\circ$, boulder hazard $< 0.30$, illumination $> 20\%$, and standoff distance $1 - 7.5\text{ km}$), extracting and ranking the **Top 3 candidate landing sites**.
5. **Rover Traverse Planning:** Builds directional cost surfaces and executes 8-connected **$A^*$ pathfinding** (benchmarked against **Dijkstra**), comparing Shortest, Safest, and Best-Illuminated routes with dynamic battery ($1.8\text{ kWh}$) and shadow power simulations.
6. **Subsurface Ice Volume Estimation:** Uses the Complex Refractive Index Model (CRIM) and L/S-band penetration depths across the top $0 - 5\text{ m}$ regolith column to report **Low ($0.27\text{ Mt}$)**, **Nominal ($2.02\text{ Mt}$)**, and **High ($6.32\text{ Mt}$)** water ice resource scenarios with sensitivity curves.

### Detailed Scientific Methodology & Limitations

#### 1. Dielectric Inversion Model (CRIM Formulation)
For a heterogeneous porous medium consisting of solid mineral grains, ice inclusions, and vacuum pore space:
$$\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{eff}}} = (1 - \phi)\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{grain}}} + f_{\text{ice}}\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{ice}}} + (\phi - f_{\text{ice}})\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{vacuum}}}$$
For dry highland anorthosite regolith at polar temperatures:
- $\epsilon_{\text{grain}} = 2.70$
- $\epsilon_{\text{ice}} = 3.15$
- $\epsilon_{\text{vacuum}} = 1.00$
- Bulk porosity $\phi \approx 0.42$

Because $\epsilon_{\text{grain}}$ and $\epsilon_{\text{ice}}$ are relatively close ($\Delta \epsilon \approx 0.45$), bulk permittivity variations alone do not create extreme dielectric contrast. Rather, it is the **extremely low loss tangent of ice ($\tan\delta_{\text{ice}} \approx 10^{-4}$ vs. $\tan\delta_{\text{regolith}} \approx 4 \times 10^{-3}$)** that enables long radar coherence lengths and multiple internal volume scatterings, triggering the Coherent Backscatter Opposition Effect (CBOE) that spikes the Circular Polarization Ratio ($\text{CPR} > 1.0$).

#### 2. Penetration Depth Discrepancy
Radar penetration depth in a dielectric medium is governed by:
$$\delta_p = \frac{\lambda \sqrt{\epsilon'}}{2\pi \epsilon''} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi \sqrt{\epsilon'} \tan\delta}$$
- **L-band ($\lambda = 0.24\text{ m}$):** In low-loss dry anorthosite ($\epsilon' = 2.7, \tan\delta = 0.0035$), $\delta_{p, L} \approx 3.7\text{ m}$.
- **S-band ($\lambda = 0.12\text{ m}$):** In the same medium, $\delta_{p, S} \approx 1.8\text{ m}$.
- **Volatile Depletion Zone:** Solar thermal models and diviner radiometer data confirm that the uppermost $0.2 - 0.8\text{ m}$ of regolith in even marginal cold traps undergoes impact gardening and slow thermal sublimation. Consequently, S-band returns are dominated by the dry upper lag, while L-band penetrates into the underlying volatile-bearing permafrost layer.

#### 3. Operational Rover Energy Management
During the $13.3\text{ h}$ shadow dash into the cold trap, the rover battery discharges from $100\%$ down to $37.7\%$. To ensure survival upon reaching the ice deposit:
1. **Stationary Sampling Operations:** The rover switches to low-power idle ($25\text{ W}$) during drill sampling.
2. **Reverse Ingress Corridor:** A return traverse back up the slumped terrace ramp to the sunlit rim requires $16.5\text{ h}$. If returning without an orbital laser power beam, the rover must recharge at a mobile solar reflector station or carry a small auxiliary Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG).
